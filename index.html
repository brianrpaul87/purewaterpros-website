How water treatment should work

Test first. Explain clearly. Recommend only what fits.

The goal is to understand the water source, symptoms, plumbing, existing equipment and desired result before recommending a system.

  1. 1
    Describe the problem

    Start with the symptom, water source and any equipment already installed.

  2. 2
    Test and inspect

    Review relevant water readings, flow, plumbing and site conditions.

  3. 3
    Compare sensible options

    Understand what each option solves, what it does not solve and the expected maintenance.

  4. 4
    Install and verify

    Confirm performance after installation and leave the system understandable to the homeowner.

Water treatment questions

Frequently asked questions.

Clear preliminary answers for Greater Victoria homeowners planning a water assessment, repair or treatment upgrade.

What water treatment services does Pure Water Pros offer?

We are currently booking reverse-osmosis drinking-water systems, existing equipment service and maintenance, water-treatment consultations, filtration and selected whole-home treatment work. More complex well-water treatment is assessed and quoted based on water testing, site conditions and scheduling capacity.

Will you work with both municipal water and private wells?

Yes. The assessment process is different for municipal and private-well water, so recommendations are based on the water source, symptoms, test results, flow requirements and equipment already installed.

Which areas does Pure Water Pros serve?

Pure Water Pros serves Greater Victoria and the West Shore, including Langford, Colwood, Victoria, View Royal and surrounding municipalities. Availability varies by service type, travel requirements and scheduling capacity.

Can I request service now?

Yes. Pure Water Pros is now accepting calls, questions and September service requests. Appointment dates and times are confirmed directly after your request is reviewed.

Do you recommend equipment before testing the water?

No. Pure Water Pros uses a test-first approach. The water, plumbing, flow and existing system should be understood before treatment equipment is recommended.
